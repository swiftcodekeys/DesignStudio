## 1. Racking vs. Stair-Stepping on Slopes

**Source:** `blog/fence-on-slope.html` — "Hills, Slopes and Uneven Terrain"

Don't let a difficult area keep you from installing an aluminum fence. There are plenty of ways to fence in your outdoor space regardless of the circumstances.

Depending on where you live, your home can be on quite uneven ground with hills, slopes, and random corners. Not all fencing materials are created equal when it comes to uneven ground. Aluminum fencing panels can really benefit any type of yard, as long as you install them correctly for the circumstances.

**The down-low on slopes:** It's nearly impossible to have a completely flat lot. Uneven terrain comes with the territory of large outdoor spaces. Varying degrees of angles can be easily navigated with aluminum fencing panels. The main part of an aluminum fence that goes in the ground is the post. With most aluminum fences, there are only posts every so often, with panels in between that don't necessarily touch the ground completely. That is why this type of fence is ideal for uneven terrain.

**Rise over run:** For larger slopes, you will need to determine the rise-over-run measurement to ensure your fence posts won't look incredibly uneven to the naked eye. To do this, you will need a sturdy board the same length as the fence panel (usually six or eight feet long), a tape measure and a level. The key is to find how many inches of difference there will be between one fence post and the next. Place your board on a sloped area and lift the end of it until a level shows you are perfectly level with the ground. Use the tape measure to see how many inches this end of the board is from the ground. Now you have your rise-over-run measurement.

**Stepped fence:** If your rise-over-run includes a big difference, a stepped fence might be the best solution for your yard. A stepped fence has the panels placed perfectly level with the ground, with each panel being a few inches lower or higher than the next, creating a stepped effect when you see the tops of the panels all in a line. However, this will not comply with swimming pool codes and many homeowners prefer not having gaps in their barrier.

**Racked fence:** A racked fence has the pickets still pointing straight up, but the rails angle to the contour of the slope based upon how much pivoting is done on-site during installation. Essentially, you can shift the panel to follow the contour of slight, medium and even steep slopes or stairs. Racked fence is a better option if you are worried about any empty space beneath the aluminum fencing panels. The pickets can stay the same distance from the ground because the rails stay parallel to the slope. This is helpful if you have pets in the yard you need to keep safe and secure.

## 2. Installation on Slopes — Standard, Rackable, and Heavy Rackable Panels

**Source:** `blog/install-on-slopes.html` — "How to Install Aluminum Fence Panels at Angles and on Stairs and Slopes"

**Standard vs. Rackable Fence Panels:** If you're looking at a modest slope that rises six inches or less for each panel, standard fence panels will work. It doesn't matter if you're putting up an industrial, residential or commercial fence. For steeper runs, you'll need rackable aluminum fence panels. These panels have a wider hole at the picket-to-rail connection, and they work well with a rise of up to 20 inches per panel. Standard pre-punched posts will work fine for this application.

**Heavy rackable fence panels** can handle a rise of up to 36 inches per panel. You can't use normal pre-punched posts with heavy rackable fence panels because the points of contact won't work with the typical insert and set method. You'll need to order blank, non-punched posts along with vertical swivel mounts and mount the angled rails to each fence panel.

**Racking limitations with accessories:**
- Puppy pickets: medium rack only, for medium slopes up to 15 inches per 6-foot wide panel.
- Decorative rings/circles: slight rack only, for slopes up to about 4 inches per 6-foot wide panel.
- Styles 7 and 8 (double pickets): limited racking because vertical pickets will touch at some point. About 12-15 inches per panel is achievable. For steeper slopes with these styles, stair-step the panels instead.

**Stair stepping:** The practice of running shorter panels horizontally up hills or stairs. You make the shorter panels yourself by cutting panels down to size on-site. This is not hard to do with an electric saw or hacksaw. You run these short panels up the slope or stairs in a staggered pattern, fastening them to posts at different heights on each side.

**Angles and curves:** You can install fence panels at almost any angle. For small angles (8-10 degrees), you can use regular pre-punched posts, angling the panel and setting it with regular line mounts. This means you can run your fence at 170 to 190 degrees. For larger angles, you'll need a corner post (pre-punched to accept panels at right angles, about 80-100 degrees). For other angles, use horizontal swivel mounts, which handle up to 35 degrees per panel-connection.

## 3. Automated / Motorized Gates

**Source:** `blog/automated-gates.html` — "What Are The Benefits Of Automated Aluminum Driveway Gates?"

Driveway gates can add the perfect finishing touch to your home, making it look smart and sophisticated. You can make your aluminum driveway gates automated by adding an operator rail to your commercial or industrial-grade aluminum driveway gates, opening and closing them at the touch of a button. It is even possible to install an operator rail yourself.

**Convenience:** Automated gates are hugely convenient. You can open and close them at the touch of a button. This means never having to leave the warmth and comfort of your car when leaving the house, which is particularly welcome in colder weather.

**Security:** One of the main reasons people choose to install driveway gates is to boost security. Automated gates give you more control over who can gain access to your property, increasing your peace of mind. This is particularly useful for families with young children or small animals. When teamed with other security measures such as alarms and CCTV, it can even help to reduce your insurance premiums.

**Aesthetics:** Driveway gates look great and give your home an advantage. The fact that you simply have to add an operator rail means you can still choose from any design of aluminum driveway gates.

**Privacy:** Automated driveway gates give you privacy at the touch of a button, keeping a property shielded from prying eyes and passers-by.

**Why aluminum?** Aluminum is not impacted by moisture and weather conditions, so it does not rust or corrode like other metals. It has a powder-coated paint finish that further strengthens it, meaning you should not have to repaint or renovate. Experts believe aluminum fencing can last for as long as 50 years. ## 4. DIY Aluminum Fence Installation

**Source:** `blog/diy-aluminum-fence.html` — "A DIY Fence Is a Real Possibility, and It's Easy to Do"

Design and build your own garden fence to improve curb appeal and ensure your home stands out in the neighborhood. Whether it's a simple structure or a more elaborate fence with intricate designs, it is a project that you can do by yourself.

**Safety first:** A DIY fence is a great way to save money but it also acts in a safety capacity, keeping children in the yard and ensuring pets are kept well away from people passing by.

**Call before you dig:** Ensure that you call the utility companies to find out where the lines are before you start to dig. Your DIY fence project could end fairly quickly if you accidentally hit a utility line. There is also the danger of being held liable for any damage caused.

**Different styles available:** A fence comes in different styles, from classic spear top to alternating spear top, and from flat top with spear to flat top with standard bottom rail. You can also choose from flat double top pickets, spear top double pickets, flat top with a flush bottom rail, or two-rail flat top.

**Erecting a fence on a slope:** Installing a fence on a sloped area means stepping the panels down in stages. The depth of the step depends on the gradient of the land.

**Source:** `aluminum-fence-installation.md` — Installation page

**Rule #1 and #2 of aluminum fence installation: Do not dig all of your post holes in advance.** Why? Because your line posts, corner posts and end posts are routed. You insert (and set) the horizontal rails into the routed holes of each post. Then, you set the panel-to-post connection with self-tapping, stainless steel screws, which go through the aluminum post and grab the horizontal rail. Aluminum fence barriers are built as you go. It's actually easier this way.

**How posts are installed:** There are several ways to install posts:
1. **Cement in ground** — Most US jurisdictions require a minimum of 2 feet below surface level; 3 feet if you have a frost-line issue. Always check your local building codes.
2. **Welded plate mount** — Mount the post on top of any hard surface using a welded plate. Posts must be 3 inches longer than the height of your fence panel or gate.
3. **Welded plate with cover** — Same as option 2 with the added plate cover to hide bolt-heads.

**Important:** If your application requires both cemented and welded plate posts, always start installation with welded plate posts as this sets the template for your entire barrier. Welded plate posts have no vertical adjustment flexibility on-site, whereas cemented posts do.

**Note:** Heavy Duty Gate Hinges require 4x4 gate posts or larger. Heavy Duty Gate Hinges will not work with smaller posts.

## 5. Pool Fence Code Requirements (BOCA)

**Source:** `blog/pool-fence-boca-guide.html` — "A Guide To Making Sure Your Pool Fence is BOCA Compliant"

If you have a residential swimming pool, safety should be your number one priority at all times. A fence surrounding your pool is a vital tool for keeping everyone safe. It can prevent children from falling into the water and drowning, keep animals safe, and help to keep the pool clean.

Although there is no country-wide pool fence law in the U.S., most states have their own pool rules. Many adhere either fully or partially to a set of rules commonly known as BOCA pool codes.

**What does BOCA mean?** BOCA stands for the Buildings Officials and Code Administrators International Inc. It is a not-for-profit agency concerned with the establishment and enforcement of building codes. Established in 1915, BOCA encourages states to adopt uniform building codes. BOCA codes can only be enforced when a city, state, or authority has formally adopted them.

**What are BOCA pool codes?** In May 1999, BOCA first drew up a set of codes related to swimming pools and safety. Included is the requirement that all outdoor private swimming pools (in-ground, above-ground, or on-ground) have a barrier in place.

**BOCA's main requirements for pool fencing:**
- A barrier that is at least 48 inches high
- Less than 4 inches of space between pickets
- Less than 4 inches between the ground and the bottom of the fence or gate panel
- Self-closing and self-locking gates that must open away from the pool
- All gates must have a locking device with a release system 54 inches above the ground
- There must be 45 inches of free vertical space between the bottom rail and the next rail up

All of these regulations are designed to stop young children from being able to gain access to the pool area alone.

**How BOCA affects DIY pool fence installation:** Before installing your new aluminum pool fence, find out what rules are in force where you live. You may be required to adhere fully to the BOCA pool safety act or part of it. When selecting panels, consider a two-rail flat top design, a flat top flush bottom rail, or a flat top with a spear flush bottom rail design, which are BOCA-compliant if set to the right height.

Many couples and families have the option of enjoying pool activities in their yard. Regardless of your weather, a good pool fence is a smart investment. ## 7. Dog / Pet / Puppy Fencing

**Source:** `blog/dog-fence.html` — "Why Aluminum Fences Are the Best Fences for Dog Owners"

It's important to dog owners that they keep their beloved pets safe. The most certain way to ensure your dog stays safe is to invest in high-quality fencing around your home and yard.

**Why aluminum?** Aluminum is a durable material that can hold up even in the worst weather. While wooden fences might blow over in high winds, aluminum fences are much less likely to be knocked down. The durability also means that unlike with a wooden or wire fence, your dog won't be able to easily dig underneath it. Additionally, dogs are unlikely to try chewing aluminum. With wooden fences, dogs can chew them, swallow a splinter, and land you with an unwell puppy and large veterinary bills.

**Busy neighborhoods:** If you live near a busy highway or in any area that sees high levels of traffic, you might want to consider investing in a commercial-grade fence, which offers an even higher level of security than a residential fence.

**High-jumping dogs:** A commercial fence can be purchased in sizes of up to six feet high, making sure that even the most energetic of big dogs won't be able to escape. Even Great Danes, the biggest of all dog breeds, can only jump over fences approximately 5 feet high. A commercial aluminum fence keeps pets safe no matter their size.

**Small dogs and puppies:** Keeping small dogs safe in the yard can be even trickier than keeping bigger dog breeds. One way to make sure your small dog or puppy can't escape is to choose an aluminum fence that includes a puppy panel. Puppy panels (also called puppy pickets or short pickets) are an optional design element fabricated directly into the gate, helping to keep your dogs in and other small animals out.

**Note:** Puppy panels are an integral part of the gate's design and cannot be added to a gate already purchased from another manufacturer.

## 8. Residential vs. Commercial vs. Industrial Fence Grades

**Source:** `blog/residential-vs-commercial-vs-industrial.html` — "Deciding Between Residential, Commercial and Industrial Fence Panels"

You can buy aluminum fence panels in three grades. Residential fence panels are fine for most homeowners, but you can upgrade to commercial if you have greater than usual traffic. Industrial panels, the highest grade, have the most metal in them. Choose these for the greatest security and heaviest use.

**Residential fence panels:** Usually the right choice for home use. Designed for typical home use, such as around gardens, yards or swimming pools. Pickets spaced at 3-13/16 inches. Double pickets available on some panels.

**Commercial fence panels:** Heavier than residential panels. They contain 20 percent more metal. The pickets and rails are bigger, so they are stronger. Well suited to light commercial and retail applications. You can use them for pool fences if you want extra strength. Standard commercial panels have 3-5/8 inch picket spacing, with 1-1/2 inch spacing on some panels.

**Industrial fence panels:** Use for the greatest security and applications with the most traffic. Designed to be tough and strong, offering the greatest protection from intruders. Use for schools, hospitals, retail centers, large estates and storage facilities. Pickets spaced at 3-11/16 inches.

**You don't have to match grade to property type.** It's not necessary to buy residential panels just because you have a residence. Many homeowners like the beefier pickets and rails on commercial panels and gates. Make a decision based on your needs.

**Pool fence note:** Specific, stringent requirements exist for pool fencing. Pool fencing is subject in most areas to the BOCA pool fence code, which requires 45 inches between horizontal rails, vertical pickets spaced at less than 4 inches, minimum gate height of 48 inches, gate must open away from the pool and be self-locking, and latch must be at least 54 inches off the ground.

**Options available for all grades:** Finials cap off vertical pickets and provide a small level of security against climbers. Decorative rings are built near the top. Scrolls are also available. Puppy pickets add double pickets at the fence's bottom. A rust-free security screen can be mounted to a frame welded into the fence panel.

**Mix and match:** It's OK to mix and match residential fencing with a commercial or industrial gate, or commercial fencing with an industrial gate. This happens often when a home or business needs a large driveway gate.
